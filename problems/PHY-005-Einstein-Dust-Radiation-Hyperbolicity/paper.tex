\begin{abstract}
We give an exact principal-symbol audit of a comoving pressureless-dust plus perfect-radiation model used in first-order Einstein--matter Cauchy reductions. For the conventional Eulerian density and velocity variables, the three-dimensional frozen matter symbol contains a size-two nilpotent dust Jordan block in every nonzero spatial direction, notwithstanding the radiation sound waves at speed $1/\sqrt3$. An algebraic, pointwise invertible change of unknowns cannot remove this block. The pressureless density equation admits an exact $H^s$ bound when the initial dust velocity is in $H^{s+1}$, and this one-derivative loss is optimal. Giving the dust a sound speed $c>0$ produces a symmetrizable block with an optimal positive-symmetrizer condition number $c^{-2}$ for $0<c\le1$; the propagator recovers the dust Jordan shear as $c\to0$. We spell out the conditional generalized-harmonic constraint subsidiary equation and distinguish a frozen-coefficient obstruction from well-posed Lagrangian Einstein--dust reductions. All claims are proved for the specified principal grammar; the weak hyperbolicity of pressureless Euler and techniques overcoming Einstein--dust derivative loss are established prior art. This is not a full coupled Einstein--dust--radiation existence theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Physical setup, reductions and exact claim limits}
Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and units in which the speed of light is one. Consider smooth Einstein equations with two separately conserved perfect-fluid components:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Einstein}
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi(T^{\mathrm d}_{\mu\nu}+T^{\mathrm r}_{\mu\nu}),
\qquad \nabla^\mu T^{\mathrm d}_{\mu\nu}=0,\quad
\nabla^\mu T^{\mathrm r}_{\mu\nu}=0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stress}
T^{\mathrm d}_{\mu\nu}=\rho_{\mathrm d}u^{\mathrm d}_\mu u^{\mathrm d}_\nu,\qquad
T^{\mathrm r}_{\mu\nu}=(\rho_{\mathrm r}+p_{\mathrm r})
u^{\mathrm r}_\mu u^{\mathrm r}_\nu+p_{\mathrm r}g_{\mu\nu},\quad
p_{\mathrm r}=\frac13\rho_{\mathrm r}.
\end{equation}
We assume a smooth comoving Friedmann--Lema\^itre--Robertson--Walker
background with $\rho_{\mathrm d}>0$, $\rho_{\mathrm r}>0$ and
$u_{\mathrm d}=u_{\mathrm r}$, and use an orthonormal local rest frame
at one event. We \emph{freeze all coefficients} at that event,
retain only first spatial derivatives of perturbation variables,
and omit background expansion and connection terms because they
are lower differential order in a standard first-order
Einstein-wave reduction.

The radiation component here is a \emph{barotropic perfect fluid}.
It is not a collisionless Boltzmann photon distribution, a
multi-moment radiative transfer closure, or a Thomson collision model.
The result concerns a \emph{specific} first-order
\emph{Eulerian} density--velocity formulation. Prior general
relativistic Lagrangian symmetric-hyperbolic formulations for
pressureless matter are known \cite{friedrich,normann};
the nontrivial density regularity hierarchy also occurs in
the Einstein--dust stability proof of Had\v{z}i\'c and Speck
\cite{hadzic}. Our restricted negative result does not
contradict those positive theorems.

Let $\delta_{\mathrm d}=\delta\rho_{\mathrm d}/\rho_{\mathrm d}$,
$\delta_{\mathrm r}=\delta\rho_{\mathrm r}/(\rho_{\mathrm r}+p_{\mathrm r})$,
and let $v_{\mathrm d},v_{\mathrm r}\in\R^3$ denote first-order
three-velocities. The positive background densities make the
normalization pointwise invertible. The frozen fluid principal
equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\delta_{\mathrm d}+\nabla\cdot v_{\mathrm d}&=0,
&\partial_t v_{\mathrm d}&=0,\\
\partial_t\delta_{\mathrm r}+\nabla\cdot v_{\mathrm r}&=0,
&\partial_t v_{\mathrm r}+\frac13\nabla\delta_{\mathrm r}&=0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
To derive these formulas, decompose
$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ into its fluid-parallel and
orthogonal projections. The parallel equation is
$u^\mu\nabla_\mu\rho+(\rho+p)\nabla_\mu u^\mu=0$;
the orthogonal equation is
$(\rho+p)u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\alpha+
(g^{\alpha\mu}+u^\alpha u^\mu)\nabla_\mu p=0$.
In the frozen rest frame, $\partial_t\delta\rho+
(\rho+p)\nabla\cdot v=0$ and
$\partial_tv+(\rho+p)^{-1}\nabla\delta p=0$.
Putting $p_{\mathrm d}=0$ and
$\delta p_{\mathrm r}=\delta\rho_{\mathrm r}/3$
gives \eqref{eq:model}.

\section{The exact three-dimensional Jordan obstruction}
Set $U=(\delta_{\mathrm d},v_{\mathrm d}^1,v_{\mathrm d}^2,v_{\mathrm d}^3,
\delta_{\mathrm r},v_{\mathrm r}^1,v_{\mathrm r}^2,v_{\mathrm r}^3)^\mathsf T$.
For a nonzero spatial covector $n\in\R^3$, the frozen
directional symbol in $\partial_tU+\sum_i A^i\partial_iU=0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol}
A(n)=A_{\mathrm d}(n)\oplus A_{\mathrm r}(n),\quad
A_{\mathrm d}(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&n^\mathsf T\\0_{3\times1}&0_{3\times3}\end{pmatrix},
\quad
A_{\mathrm r}(n)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&n^\mathsf T\\\frac13n&0_{3\times3}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The two transverse radiation velocities have zero
characteristic speed; the longitudinal radiation modes travel
with relative speeds $\pm1/\sqrt3$ for $|n|=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Eulerian principal-symbol no-go]\label{thm:jordan}
For every $n\ne0$, the matter symbol \eqref{eq:symbol}
has characteristic polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charpoly}
\det(zI-A(n))=z^6\Bigl(z^2-\frac{|n|^2}{3}\Bigr),
\qquad \dim\ker A(n)=5.
\end{equation}
Its zero characteristic root has algebraic multiplicity six,
but geometric multiplicity five. The dust block is a
nonzero nilpotent matrix of index two and cannot be
diagonalized. Thus \eqref{eq:model} is weakly but not
strongly hyperbolic as an eight-variable first-order
Eulerian system. No pointwise invertible change of the
eight unknowns can make its principal symbol diagonalizable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a unit vector $\hat n=n/|n|$, split each
three-velocity into its longitudinal scalar and
two transverse components. On the longitudinal dust
pair, the symbol is $|n|J$,
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad J\ne0,\qquad J^2=0.
\]
The two transverse dust variables have zero symbol.
The longitudinal radiation pair has
$|n|\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1/3&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
whose eigenvalues are $\pm |n|/\sqrt3$,
and the transverse radiation block contributes two more
zeros. Therefore the characteristic polynomial is
\eqref{eq:charpoly}; the zero eigenspace has
$3+2=5$ dimensions, versus algebraic multiplicity
$4+2=6$. A complex matrix with a nontrivial Jordan
chain cannot be diagonalized.

A pointwise invertible change $U=S(x)W$, with
$S$ and $S^{-1}$ bounded and with no derivatives in
the change of variables, sends the frozen principal
symbol to $S^{-1}A(n)S$. Similarity preserves
Jordan normal form and geometric multiplicities.
Hence no change of this declared class removes
the defect.
\end{proof}

In a standard first-order generalized-harmonic
Einstein reduction, the metric first derivatives
are independent evolved variables.
The Einstein wave principal matrix contains no
spatial derivatives of the perfect-fluid unknowns
because $T_{\mu\nu}$ in \eqref{eq:stress}
is algebraic in the matter fields. Conversely
the fluid connection forces contain first
derivatives of $g$ represented by those independent
metric derivative variables, \emph{without spatial
derivatives of the first-order metric variables}
in the fluid principal matrix. With a gauge source
depending on the metric but not on fluid derivatives,
the coupled frozen principal matrix is therefore
block diagonal between gravitational variables
and \eqref{eq:symbol}. It inherits the dust Jordan
block. This is a statement about that declared
Eulerian first-order reduction and not every
possible Einstein--matter gauge or variable
choice.

\section{Sharp mixed-Sobolev repair of the frozen dust subsystem}
We work on $\T^3=(\R/2\pi\Z)^3$; identical
Fourier proofs hold on $\R^3$ with suitable
Schwartz data. Write
$\|f\|_{H^s}^2=\sum_{k\in\Z^3}
(1+|k|^2)^s|\widehat f(k)|^2$
and let $s\in\R$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact propagator and optimal derivative loss]
\label{thm:loss}
For the dust equations in \eqref{eq:model},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-dust}
v_{\mathrm d}(t)=v_{\mathrm d}(0),\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm d}(t)=\delta_{\mathrm d}(0)
-t\,\nabla\cdot v_{\mathrm d}(0).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $s\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-est}
\begin{aligned}
\|\delta_{\mathrm d}(t)\|_{H^s}
&\le \|\delta_{\mathrm d}(0)\|_{H^s}
+|t|\|v_{\mathrm d}(0)\|_{H^{s+1}},\\
\|v_{\mathrm d}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}
&=\|v_{\mathrm d}(0)\|_{H^{s+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For each fixed $t\ne0$, the map on the dust
variables is unbounded from
$H^s\times(H^{s+\alpha})^3$
to $H^s\times(H^s)^3$ whenever
$\alpha<1$. Thus one additional derivative
on the initial dust velocity is \emph{necessary
and sufficient} for the direct estimate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The velocity equation integrates to
$v_{\mathrm d}(t)=v_{\mathrm d}(0)$,
and the continuity equation then integrates
to \eqref{eq:exact-dust}. The elementary
Fourier multiplier estimate
$\|\nabla\cdot v\|_{H^s}\le\|v\|_{H^{s+1}}$
gives \eqref{eq:mixed-est}.

For sharpness, set
$\delta_{\mathrm d}(0)=0$, take the
velocity pointing in the first coordinate,
and let its only nonzero Fourier coefficient
be at frequency $k_N=(N,0,0)$:
\[
\widehat{v_{\mathrm d}}(k_N,0)
=(1+N^2)^{-(s+\alpha)/2}e_1.
\]
The initial $H^{s+\alpha}$ velocity norm
equals one, while
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
\|\delta_{\mathrm d}(t)\|_{H^s}
=|t|N(1+N^2)^{-\alpha/2}
\longrightarrow\infty\quad (N\to\infty)
\end{equation}
for every $\alpha<1$.
Real initial data follow by adding the complex
conjugate at $-k_N$ with an inessential
constant normalization. This is an exact
counterexample to any common-$H^s$ estimate
for the constant-coefficient Eulerian symbol.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Radiation energy and combined estimate]
\label{prop:r-energy}
For any smooth periodic radiation data, the
linearized radiation energy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Er}
E_{\mathrm r,s}(t)=
\frac13\|\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\|_{H^s}^2+
\|v_{\mathrm r}(t)\|_{H^s}^2
\end{equation}
is conserved for every $s\in\R$.
The full frozen matter Cauchy map is therefore
bounded for finite $t$ from
\[
\mathcal X_s=
H^s_{\delta_{\mathrm d}}\times
(H^{s+1})^3_{v_{\mathrm d}}\times
H^s_{\delta_{\mathrm r}}\times
(H^s)^3_{v_{\mathrm r}}
\]
to the same space, with a bound at most
linear in $1+|t|$ in its natural sum norm.
No corresponding bound holds on the
same-index space $(H^s)^8$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply $(1-\Delta)^{s/2}$ to the radiation
equations. Multiply the density equation by
$\delta_{\mathrm r}/3$ and the velocity
equation by $v_{\mathrm r}$ and integrate.
The two integration-by-parts terms cancel:
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}E_{\mathrm r,s}
=-\frac13\langle
\delta_{\mathrm r},\nabla\cdot v_{\mathrm r}
\rangle_{H^s}
-\frac13\langle
v_{\mathrm r},\nabla\delta_{\mathrm r}
\rangle_{H^s}=0.
\]
The dust estimates follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:loss}.
The radiation and dust blocks are a direct
sum, so combining their bounds proves
the claim. The high-frequency dust
counterexample \eqref{eq:sharp} rules
out any $(H^s)^8$-bounded Cauchy
map at nonzero time.
\end{proof}

\section{Pressure regularization and the singular sound-speed limit}
Replace the dust equation of state by
$p_{\mathrm d}=c^2\rho_{\mathrm d}$
with $0<c\le1$, and normalize its
density perturbation by the fluid enthalpy,
as in the radiation sector. The
\emph{longitudinal} dust pressure-regularized
principal system becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ac}
\partial_t
\begin{pmatrix}d\\u\end{pmatrix}
+
A_c\partial_x
\begin{pmatrix}d\\u\end{pmatrix}=0,\qquad
A_c=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\c^2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here $d$ differs from the dust normalization
in \eqref{eq:model} at positive $c$
by a constant factor $1+c^2$;
this tends to one as $c\to0$.

\begin{theorem}[Best-conditioned positive symmetrizer]
\label{thm:sym}
For each $0<c\le1$ the positive
matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hc}
H_c=\operatorname{diag}(c^2,1)
\end{equation}
symmetrizes $A_c$, so the regularized
longitudinal system is symmetric hyperbolic.
Among \emph{all} positive definite
$2\times2$ Hermitian symmetrizers of
$A_c$, its Euclidean spectral condition
number is optimally small:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cond}
\boxed{\quad
\inf_{\substack{H=H^\ast>0\\HA_c=A_c^\ast H}}
\kappa_2(H)=c^{-2}.
\quad}
\end{equation}
There is no positive definite symmetrizer
at $c=0$ in these Eulerian variables.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of $A_c$ are $\pm c$.
Direct multiplication verifies
$H_cA_c=(H_cA_c)^\ast$, and
$\kappa_2(H_c)=c^{-2}$ for $c\le1$.

For an arbitrary positive definite
Hermitian matrix
$H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}
a&b\\\overline b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the equality $HA_c=A_c^\ast H$
forces $b\in\R$ and $a=c^2d$.
Both coordinate vectors are
unit Euclidean vectors, so
\[
\lambda_{\max}(H)\ge d,\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(H)\le a=c^2d,
\qquad\kappa_2(H)\ge c^{-2}.
\]
The diagonal choice attains the lower bound.
At $c=0$, the symmetry relation forces
$a=0$, contradicting strict positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact singular propagator]
\label{thm:limit}
For spatial Fourier frequency $k\in\R$,
the positive-$c$ propagator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prop}
\boxed{\quad
e^{-iktA_c}=\cos(ckt)I
-i\frac{\sin(ckt)}{c}A_c.
\quad}
\end{equation}
For fixed $k,t$, it tends to
\[
e^{-iktA_0}=
\begin{pmatrix}1&-ikt\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{as }c\downarrow0.
\]
For any fixed $t\ne0$, the operator
norm of the propagator on the common
$H^s$ density/velocity norm
diverges at least as $c^{-1}$
in the small-$c$ limit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diverge}
\sup_{N\in\Z}\|e^{-iNtA_c}\|_2
\ge\frac{\cos(c|t|/2)}{c}
\qquad\text{whenever }c|t|<\pi.
\end{equation}
Thus pressure regularization restores
strong hyperbolicity at every fixed
$c>0$, but \emph{not uniformly}
as the sound speed tends to zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $A_c^2=c^2I$, separating even
and odd terms of the matrix exponential
gives \eqref{eq:prop}. Taking
the elementary limits of sine and cosine
gives the Jordan shear.
For the lower bound choose an integer
$N$ nearest $\pi/(2c|t|)$.
Then
$|cN|t|-\pi/2|\le c|t|/2$,
so $|\sin(cNt)|\ge\cos(c|t|/2)$.
The upper-right entry of
$e^{-iNtA_c}$ has modulus
$|\sin(cNt)|/c$. An operator
norm is at least the magnitude
of any matrix entry, proving
\eqref{eq:diverge}.
\end{proof}

\section{What the generalized-harmonic constraints do and do not repair}
The principal-symbol no-go is \emph{not} a claim that
the Einstein constraints themselves fail to propagate.
Here is the conditional subsidiary identity that
must be separated from matter strong hyperbolicity.
Let $C_\mu$ be a generalized-harmonic gauge constraint,
with prescribed gauge sources independent of matter
derivatives, and consider the trace-reversed
reduced Einstein equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced}
R_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{(\mu}C_{\nu)}
=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
+8\pi\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}T\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional harmonic-constraint propagation]
\label{prop:constraint}
For any sufficiently smooth solution of
\eqref{eq:reduced} and
$\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$, the gauge
constraint satisfies the homogeneous
subsidiary wave equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsidiary}
\boxed{\quad
\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu C_\nu+
R_\nu{}^\mu C_\mu=0.
\quad}
\end{equation}
Hence if $C_\mu$ and its normal derivative
vanish on a Cauchy hypersurface, they vanish
wherever the homogeneous wave uniqueness
theorem applies. This result is \emph{conditional}
on a sufficiently regular reduced
Einstein--matter evolution and
correct compatible initial data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Trace \eqref{eq:reduced} to obtain
$R-\nabla^\alpha C_\alpha
=4\Lambda-8\pi T$.
Subtract half this trace from
\eqref{eq:reduced} and rearrange:
\[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}
+\nabla_{(\mu}C_{\nu)}
-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\alpha C_\alpha .
\]
Covariant divergence of the left side
vanishes by the contracted Bianchi identity.
The assumed matter conservation eliminates
the divergence of the stress term. The remaining
expression equals
$\tfrac12(\nabla^\alpha\nabla_\alpha C_\nu+
R_\nu{}^\alpha C_\alpha)$,
by the Ricci commutator on a one-form.
This gives \eqref{eq:subsidiary};
standard uniqueness for the linear
normally hyperbolic subsidiary operator
gives the zero solution from zero Cauchy data.
\end{proof}

The constraint wave equation is a known
property of harmonic Einstein reductions,
not a new proof of nonlinear
Einstein--matter well-posedness.
In particular it does not diagonalize
the Eulerian dust Jordan block or
supply the derivative estimates needed
to construct the smooth reduced
solution to which it would apply.

\section{Independent exact audit and research boundary}
Two independent verifiers accompany
this paper. The primary SymPy program
constructs the full $8\times8$ three-dimensional
symbol for four exactly rational unit
directions, checks the full characteristic
polynomial, null-space dimension and
minimal polynomial annihilator,
verifies the radiation symmetrizer,
and checks the complete pressure
propagator including its limit to the
Jordan shear. Its tests are exact
symbolic equalities, not floating-point
spectral fits.

The second verifier uses only the
Python standard library. Its
independently coded rational
Gaussian elimination and matrix
products check rank and nilpotency
for rational directions. It verifies
positive-sound-speed symmetrizers
at $c=1/10,1/5,1/2,1$, their
condition numbers, and the
sharp high-frequency growth
for losses below one derivative.
The finite checks support the
universal written proofs; they
do not replace the quantifiers in
Theorems~\ref{thm:jordan}--\ref{thm:limit}.

Historically, pressureless Euler weak
hyperbolicity is well known
\cite{bouchut}. Friedrich's
Lagrangian symmetric-hyperbolic
Einstein--fluid framework includes
the zero-pressure subcase
\cite{friedrich}; Normann--Valiente
Kroon provide another frame-based
Einstein--matter evolution reduction
and constraint treatment \cite{normann}.
Had\v{z}i\'c--Speck explicitly
overcome derivative-loss obstacles
in the nonlinear Einstein--dust
stability problem \cite{hadzic}.
Thus neither the original
dust Jordan structure nor the
general derivative-loss problem
is claimed to be new.

What this note adds operationally is
a compact, independently reproducible,
\emph{sharp frozen-symbol failure gate}
for a specified two-component
dust--radiation formulation,
together with a minimal mixed
regularity repair and a
singular-pressure benchmark.
It does not solve a new Einstein
existence problem, prove a theorem
for all gauge choices, describe
nonlinear dust shell-crossing, or
address photon Boltzmann hierarchy
and Thomson scattering.

\section{Precise mathematical formulation of the main result}
\label{app:formulation}
Let $s\in\R$, $t\in\R$, and work
either on the flat three-torus with
constant-coefficient equations or
at a frozen orthonormal rest-frame
point of a smooth comoving FLRW
solution of \eqref{eq:Einstein},
where both fluid densities are positive.
Let radiation be a separate,
barotropic perfect fluid with
$p_{\mathrm r}=\rho_{\mathrm r}/3$,
dust pressure vanish exactly,
and let both stress tensors
be separately covariantly conserved.
Use the state variables and
standard Eulerian first-order
principal system in \eqref{eq:model}.
For each direction $n\ne0$
the characteristic polynomial is
$z^6(z^2-|n|^2/3)$ and
$\dim\ker A(n)=5$.
Thus the symbol is not diagonalizable.
No pointwise bounded
order-zero similarity transformation
of these state variables can
remove its Jordan defect.
This is not a statement about
derivative-dependent changes,
Lagrangian coordinates,
constrained reduced phase spaces,
or different matter models.

For the exact constant-coefficient
dust system, the Cauchy operator
is bounded from
$H^s\times(H^{s+1})^3$
to itself for each finite time,
with density controlled by
\eqref{eq:mixed-est}; any replacement
$H^{s+\alpha}$ for the initial
velocity with $\alpha<1$ fails to
control the evolved density in $H^s$.
The radiation subsystem preserves
the positive energy \eqref{eq:Er}.

For the \emph{separate positive-sound-speed
longitudinal regularization}
\eqref{eq:ac}, any
$0<c\le1$ yields a diagonalizable
principal matrix and a positive
symmetrizer whose minimum possible
Euclidean condition number is
exactly $1/c^2$.
At $c=0$ no strictly positive
symmetrizer exists in these
variables; the propagator tends
pointwise in frequency to
the dust Jordan shear but is
not uniformly bounded in
frequency as $c\to0$.
These results do not assert a
constant bound for a time-dependent
or full nonlinear Einstein--matter
operator.

Finally, for any smooth reduced
Einstein solution satisfying
$\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$,
the generalized-harmonic subsidiary
equation \eqref{eq:subsidiary}
holds with its stated zero-data
conclusion. The preceding result
does not construct a solution to
which this final conditional theorem
can be applied.

\section{Derivation of the sharp single-frequency estimates}
For $k\in\Z^3$, the longitudinal
Fourier dust evolution reads
\[
\partial_t
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\delta_{\mathrm d}}\\
\widehat v_\parallel\end{pmatrix}
=-i|k|
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\delta_{\mathrm d}}\\
\widehat v_\parallel\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The nilpotency $J^2=0$
immediately gives the exact
matrix exponential
$I-i|k|tJ$, with off-diagonal
growth $|k t|$.
Transverse dust velocities
are time independent.
For radiation, the longitudinal
sound variables
$w_\pm=v_{{\mathrm r},\parallel}
\pm\delta_{\mathrm r}/\sqrt3$
satisfy $\partial_t w_\pm
\pm(1/\sqrt3)\partial_\parallel w_\pm=0$.
The transverse radiation
velocities are stationary.

The pressure-regularized matrix
has eigenvectors
$(1,\pm c)^\mathsf T$
and spectral projectors
\[
P_\pm=\frac12\left(I\pm\frac{A_c}{c}\right).
\]
The projectors become unbounded
as $c\downarrow0$,
in agreement with
Theorems~\ref{thm:sym} and~\ref{thm:limit}.
No finite-dimensional
positive-definite change of inner
product can make the family
uniformly diagonalizable in the
original zero-pressure limit.
